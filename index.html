Water-Resource Protection at the Fontaine des Gazelles Reservoir: A GIS-Based Assessment (Algeria)

An Integrated Geoscientific Framework for Surface Water Catchment Protection in Arid and Semi-Arid Regions

This book presents an integrated assessment of water-resource protection in the Fontaine des Gazelles reservoir and its contributing Oued El-Hai watershed in northeastern Algeria. Utilizing Geographic Information Systems (GIS), travel-time modeling, and spatial vulnerability mapping, it establishes differentiated protection perimeters (Immediate, Close, and Remote) to safeguard vital drinking and irrigation water supplies.

Chapter 1

Water Resources Protection: Concepts, Challenges and Approaches

Chapter 1 establishes the theoretical and environmental foundations for protecting surface water reservoirs in arid and semi-arid environments, emphasizing whole-catchment protection rather than isolated geometric buffer zones around the dam.

Key Chapter Takeaways:

  • Pollution Source Categorization: Distinguishes between point sources (wastewater outfalls, industrial discharges) and diffuse sources (agricultural runoff, soil erosion).
  • Catchment Approach: Treats the entire 1,660 km² feeding watershed as the fundamental spatial unit transferring water, sediment, and nutrients to the impoundment.
  • Conceptual Risk Chain: Based on the physical sequence: Source → Pathway → Transfer → Receptor.
  • Three-Level Protection System: Defines Immediate Protection Perimeter (IPP), Close Protection Perimeter (CPP), and Remote Protection Perimeter (RPP).

Chapter 2

The Oued El-Hai Watershed: Geographic, Environmental and Socioeconomic Setting

A comprehensive physiographic and environmental characterization of the Oued El-Hai watershed spanning Batna and Biskra provinces (including Ain Touta, El-Kantara, and Berni agricultural sectors).

Key Chapter Takeaways:

  • Geographic Location: Located in the Eastern Saharan Atlas, draining into the endorheic Chott Melghir system.
  • Fontaine des Gazelles Dam Context: Impoundment constructed to store ~55 hm³ for domestic water supply and regional irrigation.
  • Human & Anthropogenic Pressures: Population centers in Ain Touta and El-Kantara generate domestic sewage and agricultural return flows.
  • Siltation & Sedimentation: Torrential runoff from northern tributaries contributes heavy sediment loads, reducing effective reservoir storage capacity.

Chapter 3

Geological and Hydrogeological Framework of the Watershed

Detailed stratigraphic and structural mapping from Triassic evaporites to Quaternary deposits, evaluating how geological formations and fault networks govern runoff generation, infiltration, and groundwater-surface water connectivity.

Key Chapter Takeaways:

  • Stratigraphic Sequence: Triassic evaporite diapirs, Turonian and Maestrichtian carbonates, Pliocene conglomerates, and Quaternary alluvium.
  • Major Aquifer Units: Karstic Turonian limestone aquifer, Maestrichtian aquifer, and Quaternary alluvial valley-fill aquitards/aquifers.
  • Structural Controls: Tectonic faults and fold orientations (anticlines/synclines) create preferred groundwater flow paths and discharge springs into main wadi channels.

Chapter 5

Methodological Framework for Delineating Protection Perimeters

Establishes the spatially explicit GIS framework that calculates "Water Transfer Time" across the terrain rather than relying on static circular distance buffers.

Sequential GIS Processing Workflow:

  1. Digital Elevation Model (DEM): Topographic extraction of elevations, slopes, and flow gradients.
  2. D8 Flow Direction Algorithm: Routing water movement across cell neighborhoods.
  3. Flow Accumulation Modeling: Delineating flow pathways and stream channel networks.
  4. Manning Surface Roughness (n): Parameterizing hydraulic friction based on Land Use / Land Cover layers.
  5. Water Transfer Time Calculation: Integrating slope, distance, and Manning velocity equation:
    \( V = \frac{1}{n} R^{2/3} S^{1/2} \quad \implies \quad T_c = \sum \frac{d_i}{V_i} \)

Chapter 6

Application of the Protection Perimeter Concept to Fontaine des Gazelles

Practical GIS implementation for the Fontaine des Gazelles reservoir, producing the final spatially differentiated Immediate, Close, and Remote Protection Perimeters.

Zone I - IPP

Immediate Protection Zone

Directly encompasses the reservoir water body and shoreline. Strict exclusion of all human construction, agricultural dumping, or unauthorized entry.

Zone II - CPP

Close Protection Zone

Covers areas where runoff transfer time to the reservoir is less than 1 hour (< 1h). Regulates fertilizer, pesticide, and urban effluent discharges.

Zone III - RPP

Remote Protection Zone

Extends across the broader contributing catchment (travel time 1 to 45 hours). Subject to environmental monitoring and catchment-wide vigilance.

GIS Spatial Analysis & Hydrological Modeling Workflow

How surface elevation, Manning roughness, and slope gradients are integrated into spatial transfer time models

1. Flow Direction (D8)

Determines the steepest downhill routing path for surface water from each elevation cell.

2. Flow Accumulation

Calculates accumulated cell counts to locate stream channels and concentration points.

3. Surface Roughness (Manning)

Assigns roughness coefficients (n) based on Land Cover to model hydraulic resistance.

4. Water Transfer Time

Produces travel time zones enabling time-based delineation of Close and Remote protection perimeters.
